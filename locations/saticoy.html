Local Roots

Landscaping Built for Saticoy's Open Lots

Just east of Ventura, Saticoy keeps a foot firmly in the county's agricultural roots. It's a small, semi-rural community surrounded by farmland and citrus, where properties tend to sit on bigger lots than you'll find in the city. That extra land is an opportunity — room for privacy hedges, productive garden areas, sweeping drought-tolerant plantings, and outdoor living spaces that wouldn't fit on a tight suburban parcel. With the open setting comes more sun, wind, and dust, which makes smart plant selection and good irrigation especially important.

We're based right next door in Ventura, so Saticoy is practically our backyard. We design for the realities of larger, more exposed lots: windbreak and privacy hedging, hardy native and Mediterranean plantings that handle the heat, and efficient irrigation that covers more ground without wasting water. Whether you want to tame an oversized yard or turn acreage into a genuine outdoor retreat, we bring the equipment and know-how to do it right.

Local Questions

Saticoy Landscaping FAQ

🌿
Do you serve Saticoy and the surrounding area?

Yes. We work throughout Saticoy and the nearby unincorporated areas east of Ventura, including the larger-lot and semi-rural properties that define the community.

Can you landscape a large or semi-rural lot?

Absolutely — it's one of our strengths in this area. We have the equipment and experience to design and install on bigger properties, from privacy hedging and native plantings to full irrigation coverage and outdoor living areas.

How much does landscaping cost in Saticoy?

Larger lots can mean a wider range of project sizes, so cost varies a lot. We provide free estimates and can phase bigger projects so you can tackle the priorities first. You'll get clear, honest pricing before anything starts.

What's the best way to add privacy on an open lot?

Layered privacy hedging is usually the answer — fast-growing, drought-tolerant screening plants placed to block sightlines and buffer wind. We can combine hedging with fencing and trees for privacy that looks intentional rather than walled-off.

How do you keep water use down on a big yard?

We lean on drought-tolerant and native plantings, group plants by water needs, and install efficient drip and smart-controller irrigation. Artificial turf in key areas also helps cut water use on larger, sun-exposed properties.
